# Summary: Digital Image Fundamentals

This document provides an overview of the technical foundations of digital image processing, covering how images are perceived, captured, and represented mathematically.

---

### 1. Light and the Electromagnetic Spectrum

The chapter establishes the physics behind imaging by discussing the electromagnetic (EM) spectrum.

- **Properties of EM Waves:** Light is defined by its wavelength ($\lambda$), frequency ($v$), and energy ($E$). These are related by the equations:
    
    - $\lambda = c/v$ (where $c$ is the speed of light)
        
    - $E = hv$ (where $h$ is Planck's constant)
        
- **Visible Spectrum:** A narrow band ranging from approximately **0.43 $\mu m$ (violet)** to **0.79 $\mu m$ (red)**.
    
- **Achromatic vs. Chromatic:**
    
    - **Achromatic (Monochromatic):** Light void of color, where the only attribute is intensity or gray level.
        
    - **Chromatic:** Described by **radiance** (total energy), **luminance** (perceived energy), and **brightness** (subjective descriptor).
        

### 2. Image Sensing and Acquisition

Images are generated by the combination of an **illumination source** and the **reflection/absorption** of that energy by objects in a scene.

- **The Image Formation Model:** An image is represented by a two-dimensional function $f(x, y)$. Its value is determined by:
    
    - $i(x, y)$: Illumination (amount of incident light).
        
    - $r(x, y)$: Reflectance (characteristics of the object).
        
    - **Formula:** $f(x, y) = i(x, y)r(x, y)$
        
- **Sensor Types:**
    
    - **Single Sensors:** Use mechanical displacement to scan a scene.
        
    - **Sensor Strips:** Common in flatbed scanners; one dimension is captured by the strip, the other by motion.
        
    - **Sensor Arrays:** Common in digital cameras (CCD/CMOS); the entire 2D scene is captured simultaneously.
        

### 3. Image Sampling and Quantization

To process an image on a computer, a continuous scene must be converted into a digital format.

- **Sampling:** Digitizing the coordinate values (spatial resolution).
    
- **Quantization:** Digitizing the amplitude (intensity) values.
    
- **Digital Representation:** An image is stored as an $M \times N$ matrix. The individual elements are called **pixels**.
    
- **Storage Requirements:** The number of bits required to store a digital image is $b = M \times N \times k$, where $2^k$ represents the number of intensity levels.
    

### 4. Image Resolution and Quality

- **Spatial Resolution:** The density of pixels. Low spatial resolution leads to "pixelation" and loss of fine detail.
    
- **Intensity Resolution:** The number of intensity levels. Low intensity resolution can cause **false contouring**, where smooth transitions appear as distinct "ridges."
    
- **Interpolation:** The process of using known data to estimate values at unknown locations (e.g., when zooming).
    
    - **Nearest Neighbor:** Assigns the intensity of the closest pixel. Simple but causes jagged edges.
        
    - **Bilinear Interpolation:** Uses the four nearest neighbors to calculate a weighted average. It produces much smoother results with slightly more computation.
