### **Section 1: Overview**

**Q: How is a "digital image" formally defined?**
?
**A:** As a two-dimensional function, _f(x,y)_, where the coordinates (_x_ and _y_) and the amplitude (intensity or gray level) are all finite, discrete quantities.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: What is the common term for the individual elements that make up a digital image?**
?
**A:** Pixels.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: How does machine imaging differ from human vision in terms of the electromagnetic (EM) spectrum?**
?
**A:** Human vision is limited to the visible band, whereas imaging machines can cover almost the entire EM spectrum (from gamma to radio waves) and use other sources like ultrasound.

### **Section 2: The Scope of the Field**

**Q: What defines a "Low-Level Process" in digital image processing?**
?
**A:** Primitive operations where _both_ the inputs and outputs are images (e.g., noise reduction, contrast enhancement, image sharpening).
<!--SR:!2026-02-27,1,230-->

**Q: In image processing, what happens during a "Mid-Level Process"?**
?
**A:** The input is an image, but the output consists of extracted attributes (e.g., edges, object recognition, image segmentation).
<!--SR:!2026-02-27,1,230-->

**Q: What is the goal of a "High-Level Process" in computer vision?** **
?
A:** It involves cognitive functions that "make sense" of the recognized objects within an image.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

### **Section 3: Historical Origins**

**Q: What was the Bartlane cable picture transmission system (1920s) famous for?**
?
**A:** It was one of the earliest applications of image transmission, reducing the time to send pictures between London and New York from over a week to less than three hours.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: In what decade did true digital image processing begin, and what made it possible?**
?
**A:** In the early 1960s, made possible because computers finally became powerful enough.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: What early 1970s invention served as a massive milestone for image processing in medical diagnosis?**
?
**A:** Computerized axial tomography (CAT/CT scans).

**Q: What was an early major application of image processing in the space program (1964)?**
?
**A:** Processing images from space probes, such as NASA's Ranger 7 mapping the moon.

### **Section 4: Application Areas by Energy Source**

**Q: What are the primary applications of Gamma-Ray Imaging?**
?
**A:** Nuclear medicine (e.g., bone scans, PET scans) and astronomical observations.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: Name three areas where X-Ray Imaging is frequently used.**
?
**A:** Medical diagnostics (chest X-rays, angiography, CT scans), industrial inspections (finding circuit board flaws), and astronomy.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: What are common use cases for Ultraviolet (UV) Imaging?**
?
**A:** Fluorescence microscopy (to study materials or identify diseased crops) and lithography.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: Which two imaging bands are the most broadly used?**
?
**A:** Visible and Infrared Imaging.
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: What are some specific applications of Visible and Infrared Imaging?**
?
**A:** Light microscopy, remote sensing (environmental monitoring, weather), automated visual inspection in manufacturing, and law enforcement (fingerprints, license plates).
<!--SR:!2026-03-01,3,250-->

**Q: What is the unique advantage of using the Microwave Band (radar imaging)?**
?
**A:** It can collect data regardless of weather conditions or ambient lighting.
